CHAPTER III

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

This chapter presents the comprehensive methods for designing, developing, testing, and evaluating KSYN FitConnect, an enterprise multi-tenant franchise and multi-branch gym management software platform. It details the research design, requirements elicitation and gathering, system architecture and project design, development iterations, system testing and verification procedures, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 information security evaluation framework, sampling allocations, and quantitative statistical treatment of data. The methodology strictly operationalizes the Design–Develop–Test–Evaluate (DDTE) cycle established in Chapter II.

This chapter is organized as a developmental research methodology. Stakeholder consultations, participant recruitment, technical implementation, and evaluation are structured to connect theoretical franchise governance and cybersecurity principles directly to measurable software test cases and empirical evaluator trials. Actual testing execution results, performance metrics, and evaluator ratings will be reported in Chapter IV.

3.1 Research Design

The study employs a developmental research design (Richey & Klein, 2014) combined with an iterative, test-driven software engineering methodology. An iterative development approach organizes the work into sequential Design, Develop, Test, and Evaluate cycles. Descriptive statistical methods summarize requirements coverage, task completion rates, security audit checklist compliance, and technical performance measurements.

In adherence to software evaluation integrity, the study maintains a strict anti-conflation principle: subjective user satisfaction ratings are never used to establish cryptographic security, database tenant isolation, or hardware reliability. Technical security guarantees and tenant boundary enforcement are verified exclusively through automated integration tests, negative authorization injection cases, and database state assertions.

Figure 3.1. Iterative development and evaluation process

The study setting encompasses the franchise workflow of KSYN Fitness Inc. Each physical branch operates as an isolated tenant. Corporate reporting and financial consolidation utilize explicit multi-tenant query scoping rather than unrestricted database access. At least three logically separated test branches are configured in the evaluation testbed—KSYN Makati (Branch Alpha), KSYN Katipunan (Branch Beta), and KSYN Fairview (Branch Gamma)—to ensure unauthorized cross-branch data access is rigorously observable and detectable.

3.2 Requirements Gathering

3.2.1 Stakeholder Consultation

The researchers consulted seven key stakeholder cohorts representing the complete franchise operational spectrum: corporate administration, corporate financial auditors, branch owners (franchisees/investors), branch administrators, front-desk POS staff, fitness coaches, and registered gym members. Corporate participants clarified global governance, consolidated reporting, and franchise royalty rules. Branch owners defined requirements for branch EBITDA monitoring, CapEx asset depreciation ledgers, and branch staff oversight. Branch administrators detailed membership plans, coach assignments, inventory procurement, and inter-branch member transfer clearances. Front-desk staff specified turnstile monitor workflows, locker reservations with refundable deposits, POS merchandise sales, and shift cash drawer audits. Coaches and members outlined 1-on-1 personal training tracking, biometric progression logs, class reservations, and rotating optical QR access credentials.

3.3 Project Design

3.3.1 System Overview and Architecture

KSYN FitConnect utilizes a multi-tier architecture separating presentation, business logic, hardware integration, and relational data persistence. The React.js frontend presents role-authenticated views. The Express.js backend authenticates requests, resolves verified user and branch contexts, applies permissions, and executes parameterized database operations.

Figure 3.2. Proposed system architecture

The client browser is treated as untrusted. The backend extracts verified branch context from the authenticated JWT session. At the database layer, multi-tenancy is enforced through PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS) policies executing SET LOCAL app.current_tenant_id = :branch_id within each transactional connection. Missing or invalid context fails closed automatically.

3.3.6 Security and Privacy Design

Passwords are hashed using bcryptjs with an adaptive work factor of 10 and unique salts. Optical QR passes utilize dynamic HMAC-SHA256 signatures with 60-second validity windows, preventing credential forwarding. Sessions utilize HTTP-only, Secure cookies with SameSite=Strict attributes. In accordance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), all testing utilizes synthetic records, and audit logs exclude confidential member data.

Pursuant to the Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines (Republic Act No. 8293), all software architectures, algorithms, turnstile relay bridge logic, and multi-tenant RLS schemas remain the exclusive intellectual property of the student development group, licensed to the client organization for gym operational deployment and academic evaluation.

3.6 Evaluation Procedure

3.6.1 Purpose and Framework: ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Information Security Management

The system evaluation adopts the international cybersecurity standard ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (Information Security, Cybersecurity and Privacy Protection — Information Security Management Systems) and its code of practice in ISO/IEC 27002:2022. Rather than using generic consumer usability surveys, the evaluation empirically verifies whether KSYN FitConnect's software controls, multi-tenant partitioning, and turnstile hardware barriers satisfy the Annex A security control requirements across four principal themes:

  1. Technological Security Controls (Clause 8): Encompassing Control 8.5 (Secure authentication via bcrypt), Control 8.8 (Management of technical vulnerabilities via OWASP ASVS), Control 8.15 (Logging and monitoring via immutable audit trails), Control 8.20 (Network security via TLS 1.3), Control 8.24 (Use of cryptography via dynamic HMAC-SHA256 60s rotating QR passes), and Control 8.26 (Application security requirements).
  2. Organizational Controls (Clause 5): Encompassing Control 5.15 (Access control policies via 7-tier RBAC and PostgreSQL Row-Level Security), Control 5.18 (Access rights segregation), and Control 5.34 (Privacy and protection of personally identifiable information pursuant to Republic Act No. 10173).
  3. Physical Controls (Clause 7): Encompassing Control 7.1 (Physical security perimeters) and Control 7.2 (Physical entry controls via turnstile solenoid barrier relay actuation, 13.56MHz contactless RFID keycard validation, and 5-minute hardware anti-passback cooldown rules).
  4. People Security Controls (Clause 6): Encompassing Control 6.4 (Disciplinary process and credential revocation) and Control 6.5 (Information security responsibilities across roles).

3.7 Treatment of Data

3.7.1 Data Preparation and Sources

The evaluation dataset combines coded questionnaire responses, task observations, technical test results, k6 performance logs, and qualitative feedback. Missing survey responses are not imputed as favorable or coded as zero. Operational and technical findings retain separate denominators.

3.7.2 Frequency, Percentage, and Rating Mean

Percentage distributions are calculated as:

P = (f / N) × 100

Where f is the category frequency and N is the total eligible denominator.

For each questionnaire item, the frequency-weighted rating mean (WM) is calculated as:

WM = Σ(f_k × k) / Σf_k, for k = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

Where f_k is the frequency of rating k, and Σf_k is the total number of valid responses for that item.

3.7.3 Standard Deviation and Interpretation

Sample standard deviation (s) is calculated as:

s = √[ Σ(x_i - x̄)² / (n - 1) ]

Where x_i is an individual rating, x̄ is the mean, and n is the number of valid observations (n ≥ 2).

3.7.4 Technical and Task-Based Measures

Test pass rate equals passed test cases divided by executed test cases multiplied by 100. Turnstile attendance accuracy equals correct decisions divided by total scans multiplied by 100 (False Acceptance Rate FAR must equal 0.00%). Unassisted task completion rate equals unassisted completions divided by total task attempts multiplied by 100. Performance summaries report median and 95th-percentile latencies under k6 concurrency testing.

3.7.5 Qualitative Feedback, Ethics, and Reporting Limitations

Qualitative comments from interviews and task observations are coded thematically. In adherence to Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) and Republic Act No. 8293 (Intellectual Property Code), participant confidentiality is protected through de-identification, and all evaluation findings are reported descriptively without unwarranted inferential generalizations.
