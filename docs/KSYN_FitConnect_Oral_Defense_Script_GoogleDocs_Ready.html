1.2 Statement of the Real-World Problem

  • "KSYN Fitness Gym is an expanding commercial fitness franchise in the Philippines with 7 branch facilities across Metro Manila: BGC Flagship, Alphaland Makati, Fairview, Kapitolyo Pasig, Del Monte Quezon City, Alabang, and Katipunan Regis Center."
  • "During our initial stakeholder interviews and operational observations, we observed that KSYN branches were relying heavily on disconnected desktop spreadsheets, handwritten visitor logbooks, and paper receipts."
  • "This fragmented, manual system caused three critical operational headaches:
    1. Inter-Branch Data Leaks: Shared spreadsheets allowed branch managers and investors to accidentally peek at competitor branches' private sales numbers and client rosters.
    2. Front-Desk Cash Skimming: Walk-in gym-goers were paying cash, but cashiers could easily pocket the money without recording it in the ledger.
    3. Pass-Sharing Fraud: Members were taking screenshots of barcodes and QR codes and texting them to friends, letting multiple people train on a single paid membership.
  • "Furthermore, public paper logbooks at front counters exposed member phone numbers and email addresses, directly violating statutory privacy mandates under the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173)."

1.3 Essential System Boundary & Delimitation (Chapter I)

  • "Before we proceed, we wish to emphasize an essential system boundary established in Chapter I: This system is strictly a software-based web application."
  • "Physical electromechanical turnstiles, solenoid relays, and Arduino or ESP32 microcontrollers are explicitly delimited from this build."
  • "Instead, access verification is executed via responsive front-desk web camera optical scanners on counter tablets and laptops, eliminating expensive mechanical hardware breakdowns while delivering bank-grade cryptographic authentication in pure software."
STAGE 2: Chapter II (Literature Review & Security Architecture)

2.1 Multi-Tenancy & Row-Level Security (RLS)

  • "To eliminate inter-branch data leaks, Chapter II examines multi-tenant cloud software architectures."
  • "Instead of spending excessive operational costs running 7 separate databases, we implemented a Shared Database, Shared Schema with PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS)."
  • "Think of it like an apartment building: all 7 branches share the same foundation and plumbing, but each branch has its own steel deadbolt lock."
  • "Every time an Express endpoint connects to the database, our middleware injects the session variable: SET LOCAL app.current_tenant_id = [branch_id]."
  • "PostgreSQL evaluates every query at the database kernel level. Even if an Alabang cashier tries to alter a URL ID to view a BGC member, PostgreSQL filters out the record and returns 0 rows."

2.2 60-Second Dynamic Optical Passes (HMAC-SHA256)

  • "To eliminate pass-sharing fraud, we engineered HMAC-SHA256 rotating optical tokens."
  • "When a member opens their pass, the server signs a payload containing their member ID, current timestamp, and a random cryptographic nonce. The pass expires in 60 seconds."
  • "The moment the pass is scanned at the front desk, the server commits the nonce to an in-memory anti-replay cache. If someone screenshots the pass and texts it to a friend outside, the second scan triggers an immediate HTTP 409 Replay Attack Rejection."

2.3 Cash Governance (Blind Drawer Balancing)

  • "To prevent cashier cash skimming, Chapter II establishes Blind Drawer Balancing."
  • "The system conceals the running expected cash total from the cashier throughout their shift."
  • "When closing the shift, the cashier must count the physical cash in the drawer and type it in blind. Only after it is submitted does the system reveal if there is a shortage or overage, permanently locking any discrepancy in an immutable audit log."
